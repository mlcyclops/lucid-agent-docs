An [API key](/glossary/#api-key) is a long secret code that a [model provider](/glossary/#model-provider) gives you, so programs like LUCID can use its AI on your account. The [API](/glossary/#api) is the doorway programs use to talk to the provider's AI; the key is what lets LUCID through it.

Use through a key is billed by the provider, separately from any monthly subscription, usually by how much you use.

<Aside type="caution">
Treat a key like a password. Anyone who has it can use your account. Never paste it into a chat message or share it in a screenshot.
</Aside>

## Before you start

Create a key on your provider's website and copy it. If your company gave you a key, have it ready. The example on this page uses **OpenAI · ChatGPT**; every provider works the same way.

## Add your key

<Steps>

1. Click the model button at the top of the window. It shows the name of the current model.

2. At the bottom of the list, click **Add or manage providers**. The Provider Hub opens.

3. Click your provider's tile, for example **OpenAI · ChatGPT**.

4. Click in the key box next to the **Save** button. It says **Paste**, then the key's name, for example **Paste OPENAI_API_KEY…**. Paste your key.

   The row at the top of the tile, with **Add account**, is for keeping more than one named account with the same provider. You do not need it for your first key.

   LUCID shows dots instead of the key, so it stays hidden on screen.

## Check that it worked

The provider's tile now says **CONNECTED**, and the provider's models appear in the model list.

## Replace or remove a key

<Steps>

1. Open the Provider Hub and click your provider's tile.

2. To replace the key, paste the new one into the key box and click **Save**. The box shows the last four characters of the saved key, so you can tell which one is in use.

3. To remove the key, click **Clear**.

</Steps>

## Where LUCID keeps your key

LUCID stores keys on this computer, in your operating system's encrypted vault:

- **Windows:** Windows DPAPI.
- **Mac:** the macOS Keychain.
- **Linux:** the Linux Secret Service.

If your computer has no such vault, LUCID keeps keys in a file that only your user account can read, and says so. If the vault cannot store a key, nothing is saved, and LUCID asks you to check that you are signed in to your account's keychain and try again.

To see which one your computer uses, open **Settings**, click **Models & providers**, and open the **Providers** card.

## Providers that use keys only

Some providers have no subscription sign-in in LUCID. Their tiles say **ADD KEY**. Among them are **Azure · OpenAI**, **Amazon · Bedrock** and **Google Cloud · Gemini Enterprise**. Their tiles have extra boxes, such as a resource name or a project, that your administrator can tell you how to fill in.
